Realtime Ambilight

This plugin analyses the picture on the server and sends temporary realtime colours to WLED while something is playing. When playback stops it hands control back to WLED. Your WLED power limit is never changed.

1. Which TV do these LEDs belong to?

Pick the device you actually watch on; only that device drives the LEDs. Devices that are connected right now come first, then the rest by last used, so your TV is usually near the top.

5. Picture analysis

The picture is scaled down before its edge colours are measured, and a band along each side is what the LEDs follow — you set how wide that band is below. Analysis assumes 16:9, so the height follows from the width.

Default: 160 × 90. Bigger does not make the colours more accurate, because each LED already averages a whole band of pixels; it mainly costs CPU and GPU.
96 × 54 and 128 × 72 suit a low-power NAS or a Raspberry Pi. 160 × 90 is right for almost everything. 240 × 135 and above only pay off on a machine with power to spare, or if you later sample many small zones.
How often a frame is analysed. Default: 30.
There is no point going above your film's own frame rate — most films are 24 fps — or above the LED updates per second. Higher only costs more work for the same picture.

Lower gives a sharper, more literal match to the very edge of the picture, but reacts to small details and can look restless. Higher gives a calmer, more averaged glow. Default: 100%, faithful to the picture. Above that, colours are pushed away from grey without becoming brighter, which makes a night sky read as deep blue rather than pale blue. Greys and whites are unaffected at any setting.
LEDs get brighter in step with the number they are sent, but a picture does not store brightness that way: half the brightness on screen is stored as roughly three quarters. Uncorrected, a dark scene is driven about thirteen times brighter than it should be, so the LEDs stay lit through the dark, and colours look pale because the weakest channel of each colour is lifted the most.
