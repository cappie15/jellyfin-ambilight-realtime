WLED's RGBW mode is not Manual. This plugin already computes the white channel itself; WLED deriving or subtracting its own white value on top double-processes it and breaks the white channel this plugin sends.
WLED has a realtime pixel offset set. Every LED this plugin addresses is shifted by that many positions on the physical strip, silently misaligning the whole layout.
WLED's nightlight is currently active, and "force max brightness" is off, so nightlight's fading brightness is scaling this plugin's realtime output on top of everything else. Turn off the nightlight timer in WLED, or turn "force max brightness" back on.

Does your strip have a white LED?

Some strips (RGBW) have a fourth, dedicated white LED alongside red, green and blue. Turning this on sends it a real white signal instead of mixing white from red, green and blue — worth it if white looks tinted or the strip draws more current than expected on white scenes. Leave it off if your strip is plain RGB; not every strip has this fourth channel.

Off by default. Restart Jellyfin after changing this.
WLED reports a white LED on this strip. Turning the checkbox above on will use it for a truer white.
